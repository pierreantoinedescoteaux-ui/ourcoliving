# For the future essay: what each experience taught you

You asked to be reminded of this. For each stage, answer two questions:
**(a) What did I actually learn there?** **(b) How does it shape my design practice today** — design in the large sense (spaces, communities, organizations, experiences)?

- **The stone playhouse (age 5)** — first space you built and invited people into. What made people come in?
- **Organizer years (school)** — radio, dances, fairs. What did running things for others teach about what people show up for?
- **Coaching (improv + tennis, 16–20)** — holding someone's progress. What transfers to holding a house's dynamic?
- **First company (19)** — selling face to face, doing the books. What did direct contact with strangers teach?
- **The campus alliance (ULaval)** — connective tissue instead of another club; the common language (SDGs). When does a space need a facilitator instead of a leader?
- **ReImagine17 + the national youth network** — a digital/organizational space that outlived you. What made the culture self-sustaining? What did payroll-the-hard-way teach about care structures?
- **Tokyo share-house** — the year that didn't want to end. What exactly did that common room do right?
- **The houses (Growth Hub → Montréal)** — ads with stock photos, 50 interviews, a house you didn't have. What did assembling a group around a vision teach about designing for shared values? Which mistake taught the most?
- **C2** — watching rooms get aimed on purpose. Which specific design move changed how you see space?
- **The startup** — operating under pressure on something that wasn't yours to love. What did it teach about energy, and about choosing what to build?
- **The compassion fellowship** — how does contemplative practice show up in how you hold spaces now?

Then: connect each answer to one of your six pillars (co-create with humility · let the process take its time · design for shared values · strengthen with shared rituals · watch what every change does · stay highly communicative). Where a pillar has no story behind it, that's the gap to write toward.
