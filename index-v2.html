01 Intentions

I don't design for budget — I design for behavior.

01

Mindfulness
& stillness

Every house needs a place that asks nothing of you. In Vancouver I built a Japanese meditation garden — a maple, a Buddhist figure, a ring of river stones — that held the same calm through summer, autumn, and snow. Inside, in Montreal, a floor-seated room built around stillness: low light, plants, tatami, no screens.

The room that resets a high-output household
02

Community
& gathering

The thing I'm proudest of isn't a room — it's that strangers became a household. I programmed the social life that did it: brunches, live-music nights, sports, big dinners. The spaces were designed to make gathering the path of least resistance.

Where strangers became a household
03

Focused &
collaborative work

These are houses of entrepreneurs, so the spaces hold two opposite needs: heads-down focus and real collaboration. I zoned for both — quiet rooms that protect deep work, shared tables that pull people into each other's problems — so the house itself pushed everyone's goals forward.

A house that moves your work forward
