02 The proof — how I design

I don't design for budget — I design for behavior.

01

Mindfulness & stillness

Every house needs a place that asks nothing of you. In Vancouver I built a Japanese meditation garden — a maple, a Buddhist figure, a ring of river stones — and watched it hold the same calm through summer, autumn, and snow. Inside, in Montreal, I designed a floor-seated room around stillness: low light, plants, tatami, no screens demanding attention. These are the spaces that reset a high-output household.

02

Community & gathering

The thing I'm proudest of isn't a room — it's that strangers became a household. I programmed the social life that did it: brunches, live-music nights, sports, big dinners — some internal to the house, some opened to a wider circle. The spaces were designed to make gathering the path of least resistance.

03

Focused & collaborative work

These are houses of entrepreneurs, so the spaces have to hold two opposite needs: heads-down focus and real collaboration. I zoned for both — quiet rooms that protect deep work, shared tables and whiteboards that pull people into each other's problems — so the house itself pushed everyone's goals forward.
